# Research Report — Padiglione del Tè, Parco Caprotti

## Executive finding

The public evidence supports treating the building as a **small glazed neo-Renaissance pavilion from c. 1890–1891**, embedded in a **romantic/English-style garden** and repeatedly used in recent years for cultural, participatory and civic activity.

This is a strong conceptual fit for Il Portale because the building is historically small, visually permeable and already proven capable of temporary cultural activation. The evidence does **not** yet support precise architectural dimensions or a conservation-grade reconstruction.

## Historical/contextual findings

### 1. Date: unresolved by one year

Multiple 2023–2024 cultural sources describe the pavilion as built in **1890**. La Città Vegetale reports **1891**. Until a primary archival record is found, the dossier uses **c. 1890–1891**.

### 2. Style and type

Artribune and Contemporary Locus-related sources describe it as a **small glazed building in neo-Renaissance style**. This is more defensible than calling the building “Liberty”.

### 3. Landscape context

Parco Caprotti is consistently described as a romantic garden / English-style landscape, with paths, mature vegetation, grotto/rockwork, statues and pond. The pavilion is part of a picturesque sequence rather than an isolated object.

### 4. Transparency is architecturally important

Prima Bergamo quotes the pavilion as a **“transparent and diaphragmatic”** architecture. The glazing should therefore be treated as a primary design characteristic, not a secondary window treatment.

### 5. Recent cultural reuse is documented

Contemporary Locus 15 used the pavilion in 2023 for Sophie Ko’s installation and public activities. Coverage explicitly discussed the search for new functions for the pavilion. In 2026, the pavilion has also hosted a civic plant-recovery initiative. This demonstrates **reuse precedent**, not permission or institutional endorsement of Il Portale.

### 6. The site/project relationship is original, not retrospective

The 2013/2016 Il Portale paper already identifies the proposal as a **Learning City Point** at **Parco Comunale “dei Caprotti” in Bergamo**. The current work is therefore a spatial concretisation of the project’s original siting logic.

## What the research does NOT establish

The current public-source pass does not establish:

- exact external dimensions;
- exact internal dimensions;
- wall thickness;
- exact floor/ceiling build-up;
- precise stair dimensions;
- architect/designer attribution;
- definitive 1890 vs 1891 date;
- construction materials by conservation standard;
- current MEP/services capacity;
- legal/accessibility feasibility for a permanent public installation.

These must remain `UNKNOWN` or `TO VERIFY`.

## Practical conclusion for Blender

There is enough evidence to begin a **proportional architectural blockout** with medium confidence if the public image references are used systematically. There is not enough evidence to claim metric accuracy.

Recommended path:

1. create proportional blockout from visual references;
2. render five verification views;
3. compare silhouette and opening rhythm against references;
4. obtain one or more field measurements from Fabrizio;
5. rescale and calibrate;
6. only then integrate Il Portale for serious spatial evaluation.
